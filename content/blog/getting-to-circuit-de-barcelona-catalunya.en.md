# How to Get to the Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya for F1 and MotoGP

**The short answer:** on race weekends, the circuit's own advice is to take the **Rodalies commuter train (R2 Nord or R11) to Montmeló**, or the **R3 to Parets del Vallès**, then hop on the **free shuttle bus** to the gates. There are also official **Sagalés coaches from Barcelona's Estació del Nord**. Driving only works if you have **bought parking online in advance**, as it is not sold at the circuit on event days. And if you are in a group or simply want to skip the queues, a **private transfer** drops you off and collects you at the authorised point.

Below we walk through every option with guide prices (as of October 2026, based on the 2026 F1 and MotoGP events) and, just as importantly, how to get away when tens of thousands of fans all head for the exits at once.

## When are F1 and MotoGP at Montmeló?

- **Formula 1:** the 2026 race ran from 12 to 14 June as the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix. The circuit has renewed with F1 until 2032, but will **alternate with Spa-Francorchamps**: there is no F1 at Montmeló in 2027, and it returns in 2028, 2030 and 2032.
- **MotoGP:** the 2026 Catalan Grand Prix took place on 17 May (sprint on the 16th). Check the official calendar for upcoming dates.

The mobility plans described here are broadly repeated every Grand Prix weekend, but always check the plan the circuit publishes a few days before your event.

## By train + free shuttle (the recommended option)

### R2 Nord or R11 to Montmeló

Montmeló station is on the R2 Nord line (which runs through Sants and Passeig de Gràcia and out to the airport) and the R11. On a normal day the ride from Sants takes about 25–30 minutes.

For the 2026 F1 weekend, Rodalies announced **200,000 extra seats** and trains every **10–15 minutes at peak times** between Barcelona and Montmeló, with the reinforced service starting at 7:45 from Friday to Sunday. Ticket machines even showed the **Montmeló return ticket** as the first option on screen.

From the station, the circuit runs a **free shuttle bus** to the venue. For the 2025 Grand Prix it dropped fans next to Gate 6.

### R3 to Parets del Vallès

If you are staying in the north of the city or coming in on the R3, there is also a free shuttle from **Parets del Vallès** (in 2026, from Avinguda Josep Tarradellas at the corner of Carrer Ramón y Cajal).

**Bear in mind:** the train is the most predictable way in, but on the way back the queues for the shuttle and the platform can easily top an hour.

## By coach: Sagalés from Estació del Nord

Autocars Sagalés runs the official coach service from Barcelona's **Estació del Nord** bus station to a stop about 300 metres from the entrance. For F1 2026, prices per day were:

- **€16 per day** with the 3-day pass bought in advance (€48 in total).
- **€21 per day** for 1 or 2 days bought in advance.
- **€27 per day** bought on the Saturday or Sunday itself.

Sagalés also runs services from towns along the coast. It is comfortable and direct, though the return still depends on traffic.

## By car: C-17, AP-7 and parking

The circuit is reached via the **AP-7**, **C-17**, **C-33** and **C-35**. On Grand Prix weekends, checkpoints on these roads only let vehicles with parking or accreditation through to the venue.

- **Parking is online-only and must be booked ahead.** Since 2024, entry works by number-plate recognition, and no parking is sold at the circuit on event days. As a guide, car parking for the three days of F1 2026 cost around **€47 plus booking fees** [VERIFY on the official circuit website], and motorbikes parked free.
- **Follow the marshals, not your sat nav.** The circuit warns that GPS apps may give wrong directions once road closures are in place.
- **Exit-only restrictions:** at F1 2026, all access roads switched to outbound only from 16:00 on Saturday (when qualifying started) and from the start of Sunday's race. After that, you cannot drive in.

## By taxi or ride-hailing

Getting there by taxi is easy enough. Getting back is the problem: with restricted roads and thousands of people looking for a car at the same moment, finding one after the race is hard. If you go this way, ask at the circuit where the authorised pick-up point is.

## Tips for getting away after the race

1. **Do not sprint for the exit at the chequered flag.** The first wave out faces the longest queues for shuttles, platforms and roads. Watching the podium and waiting 45–60 minutes often makes the trip home far less painful.
2. **Buy your return on arrival.** Have your train or coach ticket home sorted so you do not queue twice.
3. **Agree a clear meeting point** with your group and your driver. Mobile networks can struggle with crowds this size, so fix a time and place before you split up.
4. **Bring a power bank, water and a cap.** The June sun is fierce in the grandstands, and the wait to leave is outdoors too.
5. **Careful with Sunday-night flights.** If you fly out the same evening, leave a generous margin or book a direct transfer from the circuit to the airport.
6. **Saturday counts too.** If you are driving to F1 qualifying, remember that roads go exit-only from 16:00.

## FAQ

### Is there a free shuttle bus to the Montmeló circuit?

Yes. For the F1 and MotoGP Grands Prix, the circuit runs free shuttles from Montmeló station (R2 Nord and R11) and Parets del Vallès station (R3).

### Can I buy parking at the circuit on the day?

No. Spectator parking is sold online only and in advance; entry is controlled by number-plate recognition.

### How long is the train from Barcelona to Montmeló?

About 25–30 minutes from Sants on the R2 Nord, plus the shuttle to the circuit. Expect queues on the way back.

### Will there be Formula 1 at Montmeló in 2027?

No. Barcelona will alternate with Spa-Francorchamps, with F1 returning to Montmeló in 2028, 2030 and 2032.

### Train or car: which is better?

For one or two people, the train and shuttle is usually the most practical. For groups, a vehicle with a driver saves on parking and spares anyone from driving home after a long day.
