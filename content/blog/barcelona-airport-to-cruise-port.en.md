# Barcelona Airport to Cruise Port: How to Get to Your Ship

**The short answer:** the easiest way from Barcelona–El Prat Airport to the cruise port is a **taxi**, which in 2026 charges a **fixed €46 fare between the airport and the Moll Adossat cruise pier**, all supplements included, or a **pre-booked private transfer** with a driver waiting for you in arrivals. The budget route is the **Aerobus (€7.45)** into the city followed by the **Cruise Bus** shuttle to the terminals, but allow 75–90 minutes and several changes with your luggage. By car, the drive itself usually takes 20–35 minutes depending on traffic.

Below we break down every option, what it costs (third-party prices are a guide, as of October 2026) and what to plan for if you are flying in on embarkation day or flying home straight after your cruise.

## First things first: which terminal is your ship using?

Almost every cruise from Barcelona now docks at the **Moll Adossat** (Moll Adossat 1, 08039), a long pier set apart from the city centre. It is home to terminals A, B, C, D (Palacruceros), E (Helix) and the MSC terminal. The World Trade Center's North terminal closed to cruise ships in October 2023, and the Port has announced that the South terminal will close at the end of 2026, so the WTC now handles only a handful of smaller ships.

Check your cruise documents for the terminal letter, as that is what your taxi or transfer driver will need. For a rundown of which cruise lines tend to use which terminal, see our guide to Barcelona's cruise terminals.

## Option 1: Taxi (fixed fare to Moll Adossat)

This is what most cruise passengers do, because there is nothing to book: walk out of arrivals, join the official taxi rank and you are at the pier in around half an hour.

- **Fare:** for 2026 the Barcelona Metropolitan Area (AMB) has set a fixed **T-4 rate of €46** for trips between the airport and Moll Adossat, in either direction, with all supplements included. The current fares apply from 19 January 2026 to 17 January 2027.
- **Time:** about 20–35 minutes on the road, plus however long the queue is at the rank. When several flights land together, that queue can be significant.
- **Luggage:** a standard taxi seats four with normal luggage. Four adults with full-size cruise cases may need two cabs.

**Worth knowing:** if your ship is at the WTC South terminal rather than Moll Adossat, ask the driver whether the fixed fare or the meter applies.

## Option 2: Aerobus + Cruise Bus (the cheapest way)

The public transport route works well if you are travelling light and are not in a hurry.

### Step 1: Aerobus into the city

The Aerobus leaves Terminal 1 (line A1) and Terminal 2 (line A2) every 5–10 minutes for most of the day, stopping at Plaça d'Espanya, Gran Via–Urgell, Plaça Universitat and Plaça de Catalunya. The ride to Plaça de Catalunya takes roughly 25–35 minutes.

- Single: **€7.45**; return: **€12.85** (fares in force since 1 March 2026).

### Step 2: from the centre to the Columbus Monument

The Cruise Bus does not start at Plaça de Catalunya. It leaves from the **Columbus Monument** at the bottom of La Rambla. From Plaça de Catalunya you can either walk down La Rambla (just over a kilometre of crowds and paving stones, not much fun with big cases) or take metro line L3 to Drassanes.

### Step 3: Cruise Bus (T3 / Portbus) to your terminal

This shuttle links the Columbus Monument with the cruise terminals and runs according to ship arrivals and departures.

- **Fare:** sources quote **€3–€3.50 one way** and €4.50–€5 return [VERIFY: exact 2026 fare]. Payment is usually cash on board, so carry coins or small notes; T-casual travelcards and the Barcelona Card are not valid.
- **Frequency:** roughly every 20 minutes during the day when ships are in port.

**Total journey time:** 75–90 minutes, using three different services.

**Alternatives:** the R2 Nord train (from Terminal 2) or the L9 Sud metro (airport ticket: €5.90 in 2026) also reach the centre, but mean more changes and stairs than the Aerobus.

## Option 3: Private transfer with a driver

A private transfer is a car, van or minibus booked just for your party, at a price agreed before you travel, with a driver waiting in the arrivals hall.

- **Price:** fixed when you book and charged per vehicle, not per person, so families and groups often pay less than they would for two or three taxis.
- **Time:** the same 20–35 minutes as a taxi, without the rank queue.
- **Luggage:** you choose the vehicle to fit your cases. Large families and groups sailing together can book an [XL van with driver](/en/xl-van-transfer-barcelona) or a [minibus with driver](/en/minibus-hire-barcelona).

## Tips for embarkation day

1. **Build in a buffer.** Check-in usually opens mid-morning and closes several hours before sailing; your cruise line sets the cut-off. An early-morning flight leaves plenty of time; a midday or afternoon arrival leaves very little room for delays. If you can, fly in the day before.
2. **Have your documents ready.** Passport or ID, cruise boarding pass and the luggage tags your cruise line sent you, already attached before you leave the airport.
3. **Bags are dropped at the kerb.** At the Moll Adossat terminals, cruise line staff collect checked luggage at the drop-off area. Keep only your hand luggage and documents with you.

## Tips for disembarkation day (port to airport)

1. **Time your flight carefully.** Disembarkation usually starts between 7:00 and 9:30 and can take 30 to 90 minutes, depending on whether you carry your own bags off or collect them in the terminal. As a rule of thumb, many guides suggest at least 3 hours between the ship's arrival and a European flight, and at least 4 hours for a long-haul flight.
2. **Self-disembarkation is fastest** if you can manage all your luggage; it is usually among the first groups off.
3. **The fixed taxi fare also applies from the port to the airport**, but early in the morning several thousand passengers from different ships may be leaving at once, and taxi queues grow quickly.
4. **A pre-booked transfer avoids the scramble.** Your driver waits at the port with **30 minutes of free waiting time**, and if your disembarkation group changes, just let us know.

## FAQ

### How much is a taxi from Barcelona airport to the cruise port?

In 2026 there is a fixed fare (T-4) of €46 between the airport and Moll Adossat, all supplements included, valid from 19 January 2026 to 17 January 2027. It is a per-vehicle price, not per person.

### How long does it take to get from the airport to the cruise terminals?

By car, 20–35 minutes depending on traffic. By public transport (Aerobus plus Cruise Bus), 75–90 minutes.

### Is there a direct bus from Barcelona airport to the cruise port?

There is no direct public bus. The usual route is the Aerobus to the city centre, then on to the Columbus Monument, where you catch the Cruise Bus to the terminals.

### Can I walk from the Columbus Monument to the Moll Adossat terminals?

You can, but not with luggage. The terminals are around 2–3 km from the monument along an exposed pier with no shade, a walk of 25–45 minutes depending on the terminal.
