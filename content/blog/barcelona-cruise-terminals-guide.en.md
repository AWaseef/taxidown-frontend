# Barcelona Cruise Terminals: Your Guide to Moll Adossat and the World Trade Center

**The short answer:** nearly every Barcelona cruise now sails from the **Moll Adossat** (Moll Adossat 1, 08039), a pier around 2–3 km from the Columbus Monument. It is home to the public terminals **A, B and C**, **Terminal D – Palacruceros** and **Terminal E – Helix** (both linked to Carnival Corporation) and the **MSC terminal**. At the **World Trade Center**, next to La Rambla, the North terminal closed to cruise ships in 2023 and the South terminal is due to close at the end of 2026. You reach the terminals by taxi, private transfer or the Cruise Bus from the Columbus Monument; private cars can only stop to drop off or pick up.

This guide covers what each terminal is, which cruise lines tend to use it, how to get there and how to handle your luggage. Third-party details are a guide, as of October 2026.

## The Moll Adossat terminals, one by one

The Moll Adossat is a long, narrow, unshaded pier with the terminals lined up one after another, so it pays to know exactly which one is yours: the gap between the first and the last is a long walk.

### Terminals A, B and C: the public terminals

These are run by **Creuers del Port de Barcelona**, part of Global Ports Holding, and are not tied to any one cruise line. They take ships from lines without their own terminal in Barcelona, and the allocation can change from one sailing to the next, even within the same line. If you are not sailing with a Carnival Corporation brand or MSC, you will most likely use one of these three, but always check your documents.

### Terminal D (Palacruceros)

Opened in 2007 and funded by Costa Crociere, Terminal D runs under a private concession that gives priority to **Carnival Corporation** ships. "Palacruceros" is simply the name this terminal goes by; it is not a separate building.

### Terminal E (Helix Cruise Center)

Carnival Corporation opened the Helix in May 2018. Together with Terminal D it forms the group's base on the pier, so this is where brands such as Costa, AIDA, P&O Cruises, Carnival Cruise Line, Princess, Holland America and Cunard typically operate. Whether a given ship uses D or E depends on the call.

### The MSC terminal (Terminal H)

**MSC Cruises'** own terminal, designed by Ricardo Bofill's studio, welcomed its first passengers in February 2025. It serves MSC and its luxury brand, **Explora Journeys**. Some maps and guides label it Terminal H.

### Coming soon: Terminal G

The Port awarded the seventh and final Moll Adossat terminal to a joint venture of **Royal Caribbean Group** and Cruise Terminals International, with opening planned for 2027. Until then, Royal Caribbean and Celebrity ships generally use the public terminals, although allocations vary.

## What about the World Trade Center?

The World Trade Center terminals sit right by the Columbus Monument and Drassanes, a short stroll from La Rambla. The Port closed the **North terminal** to cruise ships in October 2023, moving around 340 calls a year to the Moll Adossat, and has announced the **South terminal** will close at the end of 2026. While it remains open, it mostly handles smaller ships. If you are sailing in 2027 or later, expect to embark at the Moll Adossat unless plans change.

## At a glance: which cruise line uses which terminal

| Terminal | Operator | Typical cruise lines (a guide only) |
|---|---|---|
| A, B, C | Creuers del Port de Barcelona (public) | Lines without their own terminal (often Royal Caribbean, Celebrity, Norwegian, Oceania or Viking); varies by sailing |
| D – Palacruceros | Carnival Corporation concession | Carnival Corporation brands (Costa, AIDA, P&O, etc.) |
| E – Helix | Carnival Corporation | Carnival Corporation brands |
| MSC (H) | MSC | MSC Cruises, Explora Journeys |
| WTC South | Creuers (due to close end of 2026) | Smaller ships |

The cruise line and the Port make the final call, so your boarding pass is the only reliable reference.

### By taxi

There are taxi ranks at the terminals. From the airport, the fixed €46 fare applies (valid 19 January 2026 to 17 January 2027); from the city, it is the meter plus the port supplement.

### By Cruise Bus

The shuttle (also called the T3 or Portbus) leaves from near the Columbus Monument and only runs on cruise days, roughly every 20 minutes during the day. Payment is usually cash on board; T-casual travelcards and the Barcelona Card are not accepted. Check with the driver that it stops at your terminal.

### By car

Reach the Moll Adossat via the Ronda Litoral (B-10), following signs for "Port" and then for your terminal. The Port does **not allow long-stay parking in the cruise area**, but private cars may enter to drop off or pick up passengers and luggage. If you are leaving your car for the cruise, book a car park in advance (Parking Barcelona Port is next to Terminal E) and check rates and conditions before you go.

## Where cars drop you off (and pick you up)

Each terminal has a signed **drop-off and pick-up area** out front, marked with the terminal letter. Taxis, private cars and transfers all stop there. When you disembark, you exit the building into that same area: taxis wait at their rank and transfer drivers at the agreed meeting point.

## Luggage tips

- **Tag your bags before you leave** your hotel or the airport, using your cruise line's tags. Cruise staff collect them at the kerb and take them aboard.
- **Keep the essentials in your hand luggage:** documents, medication, chargers, swimwear and a change of clothes. Checked bags can take hours to reach your cabin.
- **On disembarkation, choose between carrying everything off yourself** (fastest) or collecting your bags in the terminal baggage hall, where they are sorted by tag colour or number.
- **Do not underestimate the volume.** Four adults with cruise-size cases rarely fit in a standard taxi. For families and groups, an [XL van with driver](/en/xl-van-transfer-barcelona) or a [minibus with driver](/en/minibus-hire-barcelona) keeps everyone together with room to spare.
- **Travelling with children?** Request child seats when you book your transfer.

## FAQ

### What is the address of the Barcelona cruise terminal?

The Moll Adossat terminals share the address Moll Adossat 1, 08039 Barcelona. What really matters is the terminal letter on your boarding pass.

### Which terminal does MSC use in Barcelona?

MSC Cruises has had its own terminal on the Moll Adossat since February 2025, shared with Explora Journeys.

### Which terminal does Royal Caribbean use in Barcelona?

Until its Terminal G opens (planned for 2027), Royal Caribbean generally uses the public terminals (A, B or C), though this can vary. Always confirm in your documents.

### Can I walk from La Rambla to the Moll Adossat?

You can, but it takes 25–45 minutes along an unshaded pier. With luggage, take a taxi, a transfer or the Cruise Bus.

### Can I park at the terminal during my cruise?

No. Cars may only stop in the cruise area to drop off or pick up. You will need to book a nearby car park.

### Do cruise ships still use the World Trade Center?

The North terminal closed to cruise ships in 2023, and the South terminal is due to close at the end of 2026.
